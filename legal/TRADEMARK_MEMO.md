# Trademark Risk Memo — the name "Corsa"

**Bottom line: HIGH risk — but not because of the car.** A non-lawyer, web-based knockout search found a **direct, on-point senior user in your exact field**. Clear this before you invest further in the "Corsa" brand or file anything.

> This is a preliminary knockout search, **not** a legal clearance opinion. Several official databases (USPTO TSDR/TESS, EUIPO eSearch) blocked direct fetches, so serial numbers and current statuses below **must** be confirmed by an attorney. Treat this as "where to point your lawyer," not the final word.

---

## The real problem: Corsa Athletics, Inc.

- **A live US fitness/training company and mobile app operating under the exact word "Corsa,"** in precisely the classes a running app files in (9, 41, 42/35).
- App Store listing is live: **id 6475633339** (Health & Fitness — workout/training-plan marketplace, workout logging, progress/PR tracking, subscriptions).
- Their **Terms of Service expressly assert trademark rights** in the "Corsa Athletics Marks and Names" and prohibit confusingly-similar use.
- Even if their federal registration isn't yet confirmed, live App Store + commercial use almost certainly creates **senior US common-law rights**, which can (a) trigger a USPTO **§2(d) likelihood-of-confusion refusal** of your application, and (b) support an opposition or infringement claim.
- "Corsa Athletics" vs. "Corsa" for fitness software/services is a textbook high-similarity / identical-field scenario — the dominant shared term is **CORSA** and the goods overlap.

**This single conflict is why the launch is HIGH risk until cleared.**

## The car (Opel / Vauxhall Corsa) is mostly NOT your problem
- It's a **Class 12 (vehicles)** mark — different goods, channels, and consumers, so it does **not** block a fitness app on ordinary confusion grounds, and the car isn't even sold in the US.
- Residual risk is **EU-only and lower-probability**: "Corsa" may be a mark "with reputation" in the EU/UK, so Stellantis could in theory invoke **Art. 8(5) EUTMR** (dilution / free-riding) against an unrelated app. Mitigate by keeping branding clearly **non-automotive** (no racing-car imagery, no "Rosso Corsa" red-car styling).

## Other "Corsa" marks — individually low risk
- **CORSA (beverages, Class 32)** — Reg. 5964995 — unrelated goods. Low.
- **ASSETTO CORSA (racing game, Class 9/41)** — US reg. reported **cancelled/dead**; composite mark. Low now (watch EU/Italy).
- **ROSSO CORSA (apparel, Class 25)**, SPARCO, CORSA GT (tires, dead), CORSA VITA — composite phrases, unrelated goods. Low — but collectively they show **"Corsa" is a crowded term**.
- Note "Corsa" is also a **dictionary word** (Italian for *race/run*). That cuts both ways: it weakens the mark's inherent distinctiveness, but gives you a **suggestive/coexistence argument** for a running app.

## Classes Corsa should file in (US/EU)
- **Class 9** — downloadable app software (the core app)
- **Class 41** — fitness/running training, coaching, instruction services
- **Class 42** — SaaS / cloud run-data processing / AI coaching (if any server-side features)
- *Optional:* **Class 35** (marketplace/subscription store), **Class 25** (future branded apparel)

## Recommended next steps (in order)
1. **Pull the Corsa Athletics record** in USPTO Trademark Search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) + TSDR — confirm live registration/application, exact classes, goods wording, filing/priority date, status. Capture their App Store first-use evidence (id 6475633339) as proof of their seniority.
2. **Engage a US trademark attorney** (and an EU/UK agent) for a real clearance opinion **before** spending more on branding. Given the direct overlap, this is the threshold step, not optional. (Foreign-domiciled applicants are *required* to use a US attorney.)
3. **Full clearance search** in both jurisdictions — USPTO + EUIPO eSearch/TMview — for "CORSA" as wordmark and substring, plus variants (KORSA, CURSA, CORSO) and the phonetic neighbor **COROS** (an endurance-wearable brand — check it). Filter to classes 9/41/42/35/25.
4. **Common-law / market search** — App Store, Google Play, web, social handles (e.g. @corsa_athletics), domains, state business registries. Examiners don't search common-law rights, but they can still defeat you.
5. **Prepare a fallback name.** Because "Corsa" is crowded *and* directly conflicted, clear 1–2 alternatives in parallel, or plan a distinctive composite (Corsa + a unique house element, stylized logo, distinct color/wordmark) to improve registrability.
6. **File intent-to-use (1(b))** in Classes 9 + 41 (and 42/35 as needed) to lock priority — **but only after** clearance shows an acceptable path. Filing into the Corsa Athletics conflict just invites a refusal and wasted fees.
7. **After launch:** set up a trademark watch (USPTO Official Gazette + EUIPO), police the App Store, and document/date your own first-use evidence.
