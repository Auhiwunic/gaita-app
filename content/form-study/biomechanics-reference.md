# Biomechanics reference — the real numbers to script from

Every episode must be accurate. These are the exact target ranges Gaita uses,
pulled from the app (`MetricGuide.swift`, `FormReferenceModel.swift`). Do not
invent numbers. If a claim is not here, it does not go in the video.

## Cadence (steps per minute, both feet)
- Good range: **165 to 190 spm**. Elites cluster near **180**.
- Slow turnover stretches the stride out front, so the foot lands ahead of the
  hips and brakes on every step, sending shock up the shins and knees.
- Highest yield fix in all of running: lift cadence 5 to 10 percent.

## Foot strike
- There is no single best strike. A heel strike **under your hips** is fine.
- What matters is **where** the foot lands, not which part touches first. Any
  strike far out in front acts as a brake.
- Forcing a forefoot strike just moves load to the calves. Lift cadence instead
  and the landing pulls back under you.

## Ground contact time (ms per step)
- Solid steady running: **under 250 ms**. Trained runners **200 to 240 ms**.
- Elite sprinters at top speed touch down for **under 100 ms**, with about
  **four times body weight** of force.
- Short contact = big force in a short window, then back in the air. Long contact
  usually means the foot landed out front and braked before it could push.

## Vertical oscillation (bounce, percent of height)
- Efficient: **under 6 percent**. Common: 6 to 10. Above 10 usually travels with
  overstriding.
- Bounce is energy spent going up instead of forward.

## Knee flexion through the stride (degrees, 0 = straight)
- Distance pace: nearly straight at contact (~15°), stance peak ~40°, **swing
  peak ~94°**.
- Sprint: swing peak ~**125°** (heel tucks toward the hip for a shorter, faster
  swing lever). This is the single most visible distance-vs-sprint difference.

## Hip flexion (degrees, forward positive)
- Distance: drives to ~**50°** forward, extends to ~**-8°** behind.
- Sprint: drives to ~**65°**, extends to ~**-15°**. Bigger range = more power.

## Ankle
- Near neutral at contact, dorsiflexes through loading to a midstance peak ~**23°**,
  then plantarflexes fast through toe off to push. Sprinters snap through it faster.

## The FOCUS insights (what Gaita flags, and the real cause)
- **Heel braking / overstride**: foot lands ahead of hips + heel-dominant wear →
  lift cadence ~8 percent so it lands under you.
- **Hip pronation**: shoe rolls inward + hip drops on landing → the cause is hip
  and glute control, not the arch. Single leg strength + cadence.
- **Heavy landing**: loud, hard contact → soft quiet feet + small cadence bump.
- **Efficient gait** (the positive): form and wear agree, balanced mechanics →
  the elite read.

## The distance vs sprint story (great content angle)
Sprinting and distance are mechanically different, and Gaita models them
separately. Sprinters: higher knee lift (~125° vs ~94° knee flexion), bigger hip
range, far shorter ground contact (<100 ms vs 200 to 240), more force per step.
Distance runners: economy over power, quicker lighter turnover, contact under the
hips. A whole sub-series can be "sprint form vs distance form, side by side."
