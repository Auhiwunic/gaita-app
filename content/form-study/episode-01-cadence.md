# Form Study 01 — "Why elite runners barely touch the ground"

**Angle:** cadence and ground contact. The most visible, most teachable, most
shareable idea in running form. One clip, one number, one fix.

**Length:** 28 to 35 seconds. **Format:** 9:16 Reel. **Sound:** voiceover or
captions only, quiet beat under it.

**Clip you need:** a side on running clip you have rights to (film yourself, a
friend, or licensed footage). Someone with clean, quick turnover reads best.

## The numbers (from `biomechanics-reference.md`, all real)
- Good cadence 165 to 190 spm, elites near 180.
- Ground contact under 250 ms steady; trained 200 to 240; elite sprinters under 100.
- The fix: cadence up 5 to 10 percent, the highest yield change in running.
- Foot lands under the hips, not out front, or it brakes.

Edit the metric frame to match the ACTUAL number Gaita gives for your clip. If it
reads 176, say 176. The honesty is the brand.

## Caption (paste under the Reel)
```
Efficient running is not about pushing harder. It is about spending less time on
the ground.

We ran this clip through Gaita frame by frame, all on the phone. Cadence came out
at 182 steps a minute, right in the range efficient runners live in (165 to 190).
The foot lands under the hips, so nothing brakes.

The fix for most runners is the same one thing: quicker, lighter steps. Aim about
5 to 10 percent above your natural turnover. Your foot pulls back under you and
the braking disappears.

What is your cadence? Film a side on clip and find out.
```
